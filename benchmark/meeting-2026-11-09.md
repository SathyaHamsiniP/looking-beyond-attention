**Status:** Preliminary, three-rule benchmark. **Scope:** operator controls, frozen-model probing, and **final-token-only** analysis of existing activation patching. No new model run, no modifications to historic code or data.

## Research question

When Qwen3.5-4B fails to identify a three-operand Boolean rule from a set of images, is the difficulty related to recognizing the diagnostic scene, aggregating evidence from a collection, or converting internal information into the correct final answer?

Rules: R1 = (A AND B) OR C; R2 = (A AND C) OR B; R3 = (B AND C) OR A. Their distinguishing one-hot configurations are respectively 001, 010, 100. Consequently **one positive diagnostic scene uniquely identifies the generating rule among these three options**; classifying that scene is not equivalent to computing the Boolean expression.

## Combined interpretation

**Supported:** Qwen's final answers are strongly biased toward particular labels/rules. A linear classifier trained on the model's hidden activations can recover scene/rule-distinguishing information much better than the model's own fixed-choice answer. Under ten-image inputs, that information is more linearly decodable at an intermediate decoder layer than at the final layer.

**Not yet supported:** that Qwen actually computes Boolean rules, that it literally *forgets* information late in the network, or that the identifiable linear-probe direction causally drives Qwen's rule choice. Existing final-token patching does not demonstrate systematic rule-specific causal transfer.

**Comparability limitations:** Experiment 01 uses 24 sets with three label rotations; Experiment 02 uses 30 other test sets with only rotation 0; Experiment 03 uses 15 held-out image sets, only six selected receivers, and three rotations. The reported percentages are **not** from an identical paired evaluation cohort. Patch rows share receivers and rotations: do not treat them as independent test examples. Shuffled-label probe controls were run with one shuffle per layer and are noisy.
